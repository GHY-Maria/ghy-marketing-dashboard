Webinar invites punch well above routine sends. Across four invites the webinar list opened at 29.9% and clicked 6.25% — well above the click rate of weekly roundups (3.90%) and more than double daily sends (2.88%). The standout was the April 20 Steel Derivative Surtax Recovery invite: 35.8% open and a 12.37% click — 1,555 clicks from a single send, the highest-clicking mass campaign of the year. Refund- and money-themed webinar topics clearly resonate with the mass audience — the January and June surtax briefings followed the same pattern. The two breaking-news tariff blasts confirm it: urgency drives opens past 34%, while unsubscribes stayed negligible throughout (0.12% overall). Keep using the mass list to fill webinar seats, and lead with concrete money-back angles where the topic allows.

The bottom line

What's working

  • Tariff urgency is the engine. The same Section 232 / IEEPA topics lead search traffic, email clicks, and webinar demand simultaneously.
  • Webinar invites are the standout email play. Four mass invites this year averaged a 6.25% click — nearly double the weekly roundup (3.90%) and more than double daily sends — and drove ~19% of all campaign clicks from just 4 of 113 sends. The April Steel Derivative Surtax Recovery invite hit a 12.37% click, the year's best.
  • Targeted email outperforms 2-to-1. Sends to specific Pipedrive client lists open at nearly double mass sends — refund-themed subject lines click best of all (W.G. McKay FTA renewal: 58% open / 30% click). The May client webinar invite opened 32.9%, ahead of the mass webinar list.
  • Canada converts best. Roughly 3× the conversion rate of US visitors — Canada-focused campaigns return the most per effort.
  • Webinars reach real buyers — repeatedly. Four invites this year, 968 registrants from ~240+ companies at the three reported sessions, an 83% show rate at the measured session, and an audience dominated by compliance and supply-chain decision-makers.

What to watch

  • Broken links: audited and major redirects in place (Jun 12). The 404 page reached 11,817 views, but the audit found ~75% came from one source — the legacy /tradelines-news/ archive (8,000 old URLs) — now fixed with bulk redirect rules, along with the moved vehicle-import guides, old .html pages, the missing logo asset, and the Section 232 worksheet downloads (now pointed to the Documents page). Still open: confirming new destinations for a dozen renamed articles. Expect 404 views to fall sharply in next month's data.
  • Search titles under-deliver. The Section 232 page alone earned 400K Google impressions but converts at 1.3%. Refreshing titles on the biggest under-clicked pages could add tens of thousands of clicks a year with no new content.
  • Newsletter signups holding at baseline (~52/month; May came in at 51). Normal — but worth a signup push while tariff interest is high.
